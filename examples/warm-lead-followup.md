# Warm lead progression — fictional scenario

All business details and history are invented. Price placeholders are deliberate; use the seller's authorised rates in a private proposal.

## 1. Cold contact — stage B

Seller: “Hi, I was looking at the fictional Oakmere Makers Studio and noticed workshops and member sessions use separate booking links. I had an idea for giving visitors one clearer route while retaining your provider. Could I send an outline?”

## 2. Interest — stage C

Prospect: “Yes, send it.”

Seller: “The idea is one workshops-and-members hub guiding visitors to the right booking step, with your provider continuing to manage bookings. Do you want to keep that provider for payments as well?”

Fulfil the request and ask one material question. Do not ask permission again.

## 3. Discovery

Prospect: “Yes. We mainly want the website journey improved.”

Seller: “Understood. I'll scope the programme pages and booking handoffs around the existing provider. How many workshop types need their own page at launch?”

Prospect: “Four. What would that cost?”

## 4. Pricing — stage D

Seller: “For four workshop pages and the connected booking journey, the fixed price is [approved amount and currency], with an estimated [delivery period] after content and access arrive. Booking and payments stay in your provider. Shall I send the agreement for approval?”

Replace all placeholders before real sending. Validate provider capabilities and capacity before promising delivery.

## 5. Follow-up

Assume the quote was actually sent on a Monday and no decision date was promised. After 2–3 business days:

Seller: “Hi, have you had a chance to review the four-page scope and quote? If those work for you, the next step is approving the agreement so we can confirm the start date.”

After a further 5–7 calendar days, shifted to a working day where appropriate:

Seller: “Hi, should I keep this project open for you, or pause it for now? Happy to pick it up later if the timing has changed.”

Respect promised decision dates instead of the default cadence. Stop after the final unanswered follow-up unless a specific agreement or relevant new event justifies contact.

## Context record before follow-up

```text

Open questions: Agreement approval and start date

Next action: Concise approval follow-up after the appropriate interval
```

**NEVER MOVE THE LEAD BACKWARDS.** Asking “Can I send the idea?” now repeats a completed stage. After approval, switch to project management.
