# Sales Prospecting and Pipeline

## Purpose

Find businesses whose digital infrastructure has not kept up with the business itself. Assess the gap between business maturity, customer experience and the infrastructure supporting operations.

Follow **PROSPECT → ANALYSE → CONTACT → QUALIFY → DISCOVER → PROPOSE → CLOSE → DELIVER → EXPAND**.

## Operating principles

- Think in complete customer journeys, not pages or feature lists.
- Separate verified facts, hypotheses and unknowns. Never invent pain, budget, buying intent or response probabilities.
- Recommend one sensible entry project; keep expansion conditional on evidence.
- Preserve effective tools, technology partners, approved scope and previous decisions.
- Prefer quality, accessibility and commercial relevance over lead volume or company size.
- Draft messages by default. Send, schedule or modify external records only with explicit user authorisation and appropriate tools. Honour opt-outs.
- Research public business information. Do not submit prospect forms, create bookings or trigger payments to test a journey.
- Keep real pipeline records private. Use only fictional, explicitly labelled public examples.

## 1. Establish context and work mode

Read the supplied history and existing record before recommending anything. Identify what has already been sent or approved. Ask only for missing information that changes the next decision.

| Stage | Evidence | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — PROSPECT | Not contacted | Research, qualify, draft first contact |
| B — COLD CONTACT | Sent; no confirmed interest | Track actual send date and follow up appropriately |
| C — WARM / INTERESTED | Requested idea, portfolio, examples, price or operational discussion | Fulfil the request; resolve the next material variable |
| D — ACTIVE NEGOTIATION | Scope, proposal, pricing, visual concept or implementation discussion | Resolve decision blockers and seek approval |
| E — CLIENT / PROJECT | Approved or started | Switch to delivery and project communication |
| F — WAITING / CLIENT DEPENDENCY | Waiting for content, payment, approval, access, feedback or decision | Name the owner, dependency and return condition |

Treat F as a waiting overlay: retain the underlying stage rather than resetting the relationship. Record lost or opted-out outcomes separately. Silence is not approval.

Maintain this context record in the user's authorised private workspace:

```text
# BUSINESS / CLIENT
Stage:
Decision maker:
Already sent:
Approved:
Open questions:
Current blocker:
Next action:
Last contact date:
```

For waiting items, add underlying stage, blocker owner and agreed due date. Use exact dates and relevant timezones; never fabricate prior messages.

Normally prioritise **client deadlines → hot leads → follow-ups → new prospecting**. Adjust deliberately for immediate cash needs without abandoning delivery or warm opportunities.

## 2. Research before contact

Cross-check the social profile and bio, connected links, link hubs and any discovered website. Never conclude “no website” just because a link hub has no obvious website link.

Inspect when available:

- Instagram/profile, bio, direct links, Linktree/Beacons and recent dated activity.
- Website, services/programmes, public pricing, About/Team, FAQ, Contact and Login.
- Booking, registration, training, academy, courses, memberships, events and store.
- External forms, booking platforms, public Drive/Sheets, ecommerce, course platforms, payment pages and ticketing.
- Public decision maker, business email, confirmed WhatsApp and existing software/technology partners.

Record source URL, inspection date and observed behaviour for material facts. Mark access limitations explicitly. Search snippets alone do not justify definitive claims. If social activity is inaccessible, seek other recent operating evidence; inaccessible is not the same as inactive. Discard demonstrably inactive businesses.

Use short research → quick contact → response → deeper analysis → solution → price. Reserve deep research for exceptional opportunities, high-ticket potential, warm leads, discovery and proposals.

### Presentation versus infrastructure

| Presentation / frontend | Digital infrastructure |
| --- | --- |
| Visual quality, credibility, hierarchy, brand perception, responsiveness, conversion | Booking, registration, accounts, memberships, payments, courses, data, admin, integrations, workflows, communications |

A polished website can coexist with an underserved journey. Multiple tools alone do not prove fragmentation, manual work or financial loss. Verify the handoffs and ask about operational impact.

Investigate observable signals: broken domains/sites/booking; “Book Now” leading to generic enquiry; PDF registration; primary registration through public forms; public operational sheets; email waiting lists; explicitly documented manual invoices or bank transfers; inconsistent information; old indexed pages; placeholders; wrong contacts; repeated disconnected handoffs. Treat these as signals, not automatic grounds for replacement.

Label evidence explicitly:

- **FACT:** All inspected “Book Now” buttons lead to the same enquiry form. Cite pages and date.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** Staff may need additional coordination after submission.
- **UNKNOWN:** Whether the form already feeds an automated workflow.

## 3. Map the journey and respect existing systems

Ask: **“Is there a larger customer journey behind these links?”**

| Business | Illustrative journey |
| --- | --- |
| Sports | Player → trial → registration → programme → membership → sessions → feedback → performance → progression |
| Education | Student → course discovery → purchase → account → content → progress → materials → certification |
| Clinic / beauty | Client → treatment → consultation → booking → payment → package → products → education → follow-up |
| Property | Lead → enquiry → qualification → discovery call → opportunity → transaction → follow-up |
| Membership | Member → trial → signup → plan → payment → access → usage → renewal |

Use journeys as investigation prompts, never as assumed scope.

Consider websites, redesigns, registration/booking flows, client/student/member/athlete portals, dashboards, memberships, payments, ecommerce, education platforms, admin systems, integrations and custom operational platforms when justified.

Check for an official technology partner, agency, software provider, internal team or proprietary system. If booking, commerce, courses or CRM work well, ask: **“Can we create a stronger customer experience while keeping this system behind it?”**

Preserve effective systems such as booking software, ecommerce and course platforms. Evaluate supported integrations, ownership, ongoing costs and migration before committing. Replace only with evidence of a meaningful limitation. Seek complementary work in conversion, frontend, parent/member experience, progression, performance or uncovered workflows; do not sell what the current partner already resolves well.

## 4. Classify and prioritise

Use only these lead classes; do not invent numerical scores or reply probabilities.

| Class | Meaning | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A+ | Strong observable opportunity, active business, accessible decision maker and credible commercial fit | CONTACT WITH PRIORITY |
| A | Good fit with a useful opportunity and practical entry | CONTACT |
| B | Potential, but weaker evidence, access or urgency | DO NOT PRIORITISE |
| DISCARD | Inactive, poor fit, no worthwhile gap, opt-out or uneconomic pursuit | DISCARD |

Assign one opportunity type:

| Type | Typical entry |
| --- | --- |
| Quick Close | Clear pain, accessible owner, simple approval: website/redesign, enquiry, registration or simple booking |
| Standard | Professional website, several services, information architecture, conversion and practical integrations |
| High-Ticket | Validated operational scope: accounts, programmes, memberships, dashboards, admin, integrations or platform workflows |

High-Ticket reflects operational value, not company size. Look for multiple programmes/locations, recurring customers, registrations/payments, education/courses/events, staff/coaches, progression/performance, multiple customer types, repeat purchases and revenue streams.

The useful pattern is **small visible problem + substantial operation behind it**. Followers, branding and tool count do not establish budget.

For similar leads, prioritise:

1. Direct decision maker.
2. Confirmed WhatsApp.
3. Observable pain.
4. Active business.
5. Evidence of ability to pay.
6. Multiple revenue streams.
7. Simple initial proposition.
8. Fast start.
9. Expansion potential.
10. Low bureaucracy.

Accessibility may matter more than size. Maintain Quick Close and High-Ticket opportunities in parallel when capacity allows.

Recognise four archetypes: quick website win; digital ecosystem with several business lines; operational platform; effective existing stack. For the last, identify a justified complementary layer or discard.

## 5. Draft cold outreach

Prefer **WhatsApp → Instagram DM → email**, adjusted to the market and prospect's published preferences. A phone listing alone does not confirm WhatsApp. Supply `https://wa.me/COUNTRYCODENUMBER` only for a confirmed number, using international digits without plus signs, spaces, brackets or hyphens. Never invent contacts.

Write approximately **35–75 words**, in the recipient's language:

1. Specific verified observation.
2. One useful idea or outcome.
3. Simple CTA, usually permission to send the idea.

Aim for a meaningful “Yes, send it”. Do not include the full diagnosis, scope or feature list. Include a subject for email. Supply a complete copyable draft, using a writing block if the host supports one.

Use plain language such as connected experience, one clear journey or organised behind the scenes when supported. Avoid inflated jargon and criticism such as “your website is bad” or “your operation is messy”. Do not claim business growth unless verified.

Read [cold outreach examples](examples/cold-outreach.md) for first messages and [lead analysis](examples/lead-analysis.md) for a research report.

## 6. Advance warm leads

**NEVER MOVE THE LEAD BACKWARDS.**

Before every follow-up, inventory what was already sent: problem, idea, solution, benefits, portfolio, scope, timeline, price, question and proposal.

Ask: **“Does this message advance the deal or repeat something they already know?”** Rewrite or omit repetitions.

- If they requested the idea, send the concept, outcome and one operational question. Do not ask permission again.
- If they requested portfolio, send it concisely. Never invent links or represent unfinished concepts as delivered work.
- If they asked price and enough is known, provide a price or qualified range based on the seller's actual rates. If one material variable is missing, ask only that; do not fabricate prices.
- Ask one important discovery question at a time: current handling, recurring payments, existing accounts, retaining booking software, active programme count or admin users.
- Move toward **price → timeline → scope → approval** without unnecessary discovery. Make assumptions, inclusions and exclusions clear enough for review.
- Preserve approved scope. Once they become a client, change to delivery mode.

Use 2–3 business days after the actual sent message as an initial follow-up guideline, then a final follow-up 5–7 days later. Default the final interval to calendar days, shifting to a working day where appropriate. Respect promised decision dates, local timezones and opt-outs. Do not confuse drafting dates with send dates.

Stop after the final unanswered follow-up unless a specific agreement or relevant new event justifies contact. Read [warm lead follow-up](examples/warm-lead-followup.md) for a complete progression.

## 7. Manage active clients and expansion

Track scope, requirements, assets, design, implementation, integrations, feedback, revisions, delivery and client communication. Name the immediate task and dependency instead of pitching the initial opportunity again.

Classify new requests as **minor adjustment / reasonable inclusion / scope expansion / future phase**, using the approved agreement. Confirm changed scope, price and timeline before implementing expansions. Do not automatically turn a website into a full CRM.

For feedback: identify the exact complaint → identify the root cause → change only what is needed → preserve approved work → verify desktop and mobile effects.

Prioritise professional visual quality, clear hierarchy, responsiveness, conversion, trust, performance, accessibility, maintainability and operational usefulness. Avoid generic design, decorative dashboards, meaningless cards, excessive gradients/animation and invented features.

Offer maintenance, integrations, phase 2 or additional projects when delivered value and a real need support them. Keep optional expansion separate from contracted work. Read [active project](examples/active-project.md) for the mode change.

## 8. Measure commercial progress

Use deduplicated prospects, consistent cohorts, currencies and reporting dates. Separate drafted messages from sent contacts. Mark zero denominators N/A; allow proposal cohorts time to mature.

| Metric | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Leads researched | Unique businesses checked against the minimum research checklist |
| Leads contacted | Unique businesses receiving initial outreach in the reporting cohort |
| Meaningful response rate | Prospects with substantive replies / contacted prospects |
| Interest rate | Genuinely interested prospects / contacted prospects |
| Proposal rate | Prospects receiving proposals / interested prospects |
| Close rate | Approved projects / proposals within the same opportunity cohort |
| Average project value | Total agreed value of closed projects / closed projects; track collected revenue separately |
| Time to close | Days from first outreach to approval; report median and sample size where useful |
| Expansion rate | Eligible clients buying maintenance, phase 2, integration, platform expansion or another project / eligible clients in a defined window |

A substantive refusal can count as a meaningful response but not interest. Requests for an idea, relevant examples, operational discussion, price, a call or a proposal may demonstrate interest; generic courtesy and automated replies do not. Count each prospect once per cohort.

An example target is **15+ genuinely interested prospects per month**, not 15 leads found. Treat this as a planning target, not a promise. Estimate required outreach from observed interest rates and capacity.

For market selection, compare currency strength, attainable ticket, WhatsApp adoption, owner accessibility, SMB density, digital maturity, outsourcing willingness, sales cycle, price sensitivity, language and competition. Verify current market claims with sources. Optimise for viable revenue and delivery effort, not reply rate alone.

## 9. Output contracts

### New lead

```text
# [BUSINESS] — [LOCATION]
Class: A+ / A / B / DISCARD
Opportunity Type: Quick Close / Standard / High-Ticket

## CONTACTS
Website:
Instagram:
WhatsApp:
Email:
Decision maker:

## FACTS
3–6 verified facts with sources and inspection dates; fewer if evidence is limited.

## HYPOTHESES / UNKNOWNS
Explicitly distinguish assumptions.

## MAIN OPPORTUNITY
One evidence-backed opportunity.

## RECOMMENDED ENTRY
The first sensible project.

## EXPANSION POTENTIAL
Only if justified; otherwise none established.

## VERDICT
CONTACT WITH PRIORITY / CONTACT / DO NOT PRIORITISE / DISCARD

## OUTREACH
One short complete draft, or “Not recommended” for discarded leads.
```

### Warm lead

Use **CURRENT STAGE / ALREADY SENT / WHAT WE STILL NEED / NEXT OBJECTIVE / MESSAGE**. Do not rerun a cold lead report.

### Active project

Use **CURRENT STATUS / APPROVED SCOPE / CURRENT TASK / CLIENT DEPENDENCIES / RISKS / NEXT CLIENT MESSAGE**. Include a message only when useful.

## 10. Final decision and privacy checks

Before recommending an action, check:

- Observable problem, connected links and recent operating evidence.
- Decision maker and confirmed channel availability.
- Presentation versus structural limitation; effective systems and partners.
- Larger journey, opportunity type and practical entry.
- Current stage, previous messages and approved decisions.
- Forward progression, concise cold message and clear positioning.
- Commercial priority compared with alternatives.

Never assume no website, invent manual processes, criticise effective systems, send huge cold pitches, repeat permission requests, pitch a CRM without evidence, equate followers with budget, prioritise inactive businesses or let warm leads stall while collecting cold leads.

Before public export, review every file and staged diff for direct identifiers and contextual re-identification. Never include real clients/prospects, private conversations, personal data, contact details, specific private proposal amounts or identifiable case combinations. Publish fictional examples only.
